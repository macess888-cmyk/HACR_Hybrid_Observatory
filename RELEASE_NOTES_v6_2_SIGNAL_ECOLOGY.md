# Release Summary

Version:

```text
v6.2-signal-ecology
```

This release introduces Signal Ecology as a first-class architectural layer within the HACR Hybrid Observatory.

The purpose of this release is not to expand authority, governance, prediction capability, or execution capability.

The purpose of this release is to improve architectural clarity regarding:

* signals
* localization
* inspectability
* formation visibility
* observer-only boundaries

This release represents an architectural stabilization rather than an architectural expansion.

# Why This Release Exists

Earlier repository models primarily focused on:

```text

survivability

environment
time
```

These models successfully improved visibility into persistence, interruption realism, environmental visibility, and temporal observability.

However, a recurring question emerged:

```text

```

The Signal Ecology architecture was introduced to provide a clearer answer.

The Observatory does not observe meaning.

The Observatory does not observe authority.

The Observatory does not observe decisions.

The Observatory observes signals operating within a broader signal ecology.

# New Boundary Clarifications

Several important distinctions were formalized.

## Signal ≠ Interpretation

Signals are observable traces.

Signals are not meanings.

Signals are not conclusions.

Signals are not decisions.

---

## Localization ≠ Meaning

Localization identifies where observable traces can be inspected.

Localization does not establish meaning.

Localization does not establish authority.

---

## Visibility ≠ Authority

Visibility may increase inspectability.

Visibility does not create authority.

Visibility does not create legitimacy.

Visibility does not create governance.

---

## Inspectability ≠ Control

Inspectability preserves the ability to review and examine formations.

Inspectability does not provide intervention capability.

Inspectability does not provide execution capability.

---

## Formation Visibility ≠ Decision Support

Formation visibility preserves visibility into emerging formations.

Formation visibility does not prescribe action.

Formation visibility does not determine priority.

Formation visibility does not determine response.
