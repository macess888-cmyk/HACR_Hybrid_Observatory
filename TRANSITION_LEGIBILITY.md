# TRANSITION LEGIBILITY

## PURPOSE

This document defines observer-side transition legibility concepts
used by the HACR Hybrid Observatory.

Transition legibility concerns whether system state change remains:
- observable
- bounded
- interpretable
- recoverable

under stress, degradation, interruption, or topology change.

This document is observational only.

---

# CORE REDUCTION

Systems become more dangerous when transition behavior
stops being legible before collapse becomes visible.

---

# LEGIBLE TRANSITION BEHAVIOR (LTB)

## Definition

Transition behavior that remains:
- observable
- bounded
- interpretable
- recoverable

during stress or state change.

---

## OBSERVATIONAL PURPOSE

LTB is used to inspect:
- graceful degradation
- continuity visibility
- transition predictability
- recoverability viability
- interruption visibility
- topology-local transition behavior

LTB does not imply:
- safety
- correctness
- authorization
- operational validity

---

# TRANSITION DEGRADATION

Transition degradation occurs when:
- visibility decreases
- recoverability becomes uncertain
- transition boundaries become unclear
- replayability weakens
- interruption viability degrades
- topology interpretation becomes unstable

Transition degradation may occur before visible failure.

---

# LEGIBILITY LOSS CONDITIONS

Legibility loss may include:
- hidden dependency propagation
- delayed survivability emergence
- fragmented replay visibility
- incomplete topology observation
- stale state assumptions
- compressed transition intervals
- irreversible edge proximity

Legibility loss increases uncertainty.

---

# GRACEFUL FAILURE BOUNDARY (GFB)

Observer-side preference:
- early failure visibility
- gradual degradation
- bounded transition corridors
- recoverability preservation
- safe-state reversion potential

The Observatory does not optimize systems
toward irreversible edge behavior.

---

# OBSERVER BOUNDARY

The Observatory:
- observes
- classifies
- maps
- visualizes
- pressure-tests

The Observatory does not:
- govern
- authorize
- operationalize
- certify
- optimize execution

---

# NON-CLAIM

Transition legibility observations:
- do not guarantee containment
- do not guarantee interruption success
- do not guarantee future recoverability
- do not imply execution validity

All observations remain bounded to:
- visible topology
- available evidence
- supplied inputs
- local inspection conditions

Unknown downstream conditions may remain possible.
