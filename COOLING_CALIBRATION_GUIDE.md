This guide explains how cooling stack outputs should be interpreted during bounded observability review.

Cooling outputs are diagnostic only.

- authorize
- certify
- validate truth
- determine legitimacy
- invalidate artifacts
- prescribe intervention

UNKNOWN -> HOLD.

# Cooling Output Semantics

## PASS

Meaning:

No major semantic survivability pressure localized under current inspection conditions.

PASS does not imply:

- correctness
- truth
- safety
- legitimacy
- operational validity

PASS only means:
bounded pressure was not strongly localized during the current run.

---

## COOL

Meaning:

Semantic or interpretive pressure localized.

Examples:

- semantic density accumulation
- visual authority drift
- recursive importance escalation
- external interpretation pressure
- falsifiability degradation risk

COOL does not imply failure.

COOL means:
bounded review recommended.

---

## HOLD

Meaning:

Pressure remains unresolved or insufficiently localized.

HOLD preserves bounded uncertainty.

HOLD does not imply:

- failure
- approval
- correctness
- rejection

HOLD prevents premature semantic closure.

---

## STOP

Meaning:

Strong bounded review recommended before broader public use.

Examples:

- governance-authority interpretation drift
- prediction-engine framing
- ideology drift
- self-authorizing recursion
- strong falsifiability erosion

STOP does not determine truth or invalidity.

STOP localizes elevated semantic survivability pressure.

---

## REWRITE

Meaning:

Bounded reformulation recommended.

Usually triggered by:

- semantic overload
- interpretive instability
- authority inheritance pressure
- excessive explanatory density

REWRITE does not invalidate the underlying artifact.
