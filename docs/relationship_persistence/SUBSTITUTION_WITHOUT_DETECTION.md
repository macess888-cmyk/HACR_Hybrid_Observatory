## Motivation

Many continuity analyses assume that if an object appears
continuous, the underlying relationships supporting that object
have also remained continuous.

This may not always be true.

A relationship may be replaced while continuity language,
identity claims, object descriptions, or operational outputs
remain stable.

The purpose of this note is to explore that possibility.

---

## Core Question

When a system appears continuous,

how do we distinguish:

Preservation

from

Successful Replacement?

---

## Candidate Reduction

Preservation
=
The same relationship continues.

Replacement
=
A new relationship occupies the position
of a previous relationship.

Substitution Without Detection
=
Replacement occurs while continuity is still assumed.

## Inspection Questions

What relationship originally made the object meaningful?

What relationship currently makes the object operational?

Are they the same relationship?

If not:

When did substitution occur?

Was substitution declared?

Was identity revalidated?

Did descriptions remain unchanged?

---

## Preservation vs Replacement

A system may report:

- preserved
- stable
- governed
- certified
- trusted
- authorized

while the underlying relationship has changed.

The objective is not to determine whether
the replacement is good or bad.

The objective is to determine whether
replacement occurred.

---

## Relationship Persistence Connection

Relationship Persistence asks:

Did the relationship persist?

Substitution Without Detection asks:

Which relationship persisted?

The questions are related but not identical.
