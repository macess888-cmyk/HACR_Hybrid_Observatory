## Motivation

Many systems appear to assume that:

- formation order
- failure order
- detection order

are equivalent.

This may not be true.

A system may form through one sequence,
fail through another sequence,
and be detected through a third sequence.

The purpose of this note is to explore that possibility.

---

## Candidate Observation

Formation Order
≠
Failure Order
≠
Detection Order

---

## Candidate Formation Geometry

Dependency
↓
Relationship
↓
Identity
↓
Object
↓
Description

Interpretation:

Dependencies enable relationships.

Relationships contribute to identity.

Identity stabilizes into an object.

Objects acquire descriptions.

---

## Candidate Failure Geometry

Relationship Weakening
↓
Dependency Substitution
↓
Identity Drift
↓
Object Drift
↓
Description Persistence

Interpretation:

The earliest degradation may occur within
relationships and dependencies.

Descriptions may survive long after underlying
conditions change.

---

## Candidate Detection Geometry

Description
↓
Object
↓
Identity

Interpretation:

Many systems primarily monitor visible objects,
states, outputs, labels, or descriptions.

Relationship-level degradation may remain invisible.

---

## Core Asymmetry

Formation may begin deep.

Failure may begin in the middle.

Detection may occur at the surface.

## Counterexamples

Relationship change
≠
Relationship failure

Biological systems continuously replace and
reconfigure relationships while preserving identity.

Therefore:

Relationship persistence must not be reduced
to relationship immobility.

Possible relationship transitions include:

- strengthening
- weakening
- substitution
- reconfiguration
- fragmentation
- reconstruction
