# PASS / HOLD / FAIL SEMANTICS

## PASS

Bounded diagnostic conditions reproduced under inspection.

PASS does not mean:

- certified safe
- operationally authorized
- globally complete
- governance-valid
- future-proof

PASS only means:

Observed conditions did not reproduce survivable continuation under inspected constraints.

---

## HOLD

Insufficient visibility for operational closure.

Examples:

- unknown topology
- unclear carrier inventory
- replay uncertainty
- unresolved residue inertness
- unclear interruption realism
- incomplete continuation visibility

HOLD preserves fail-closed behavior.

Unknown consequence carriers remain operationally equivalent to survivable consequence.

---

## FAIL

Observed survivability or continuation persistence under inspected conditions.

Examples:

- replay-capable continuation
- persistence escape
- residue survivability
- hidden carrier survivability
- fragmented continuation
- interruption-local failure
- operationally reachable continuation after invalidity

FAIL is diagnostic only.

FAIL is not:

- legal judgment
- moral judgment
- institutional determination
- enforcement instruction

# ENGINEERING SAFETY CHECKLIST

Before adding new repository capabilities:

- Is the feature observer-only?
- Is the feature removable?
- Is the feature reproducible?
- Is the feature falsifiable?
- Does HOLD remain preserved?
- Can outputs become execution-consumable?
- Does the feature introduce authority implication?
- Does the feature create workflow dependency?
- Does the feature survive observer removal?
- Does the feature increase hidden operational centrality?

If uncertainty remains:

-> HOLD
