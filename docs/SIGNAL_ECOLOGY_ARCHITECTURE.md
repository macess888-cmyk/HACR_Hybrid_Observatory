# Signal Ecology Architecture

## Purpose

This document defines the signal ecology layer of the HACR Hybrid Observatory.

The Observatory is not the source of reality.

The Observatory is not the source of meaning.

The Observatory operates within a broader signal ecology.

Its role is to preserve signal localization, inspectability, localization capacity, and distinction visibility while remaining outside interpretation, authority, decision, action, and consequence.

# Reality

Reality is the dynamic environment in which conditions, events, relationships, absences, patterns, and changes occur.

Reality is not controlled by the Observatory.

Reality is not produced by the Observatory.

Reality may generate observable traces.

---

# Signals

Signals are observable traces that may contribute to formation visibility.

Signals may include:

- events
- conditions
- measurements
- behaviors
- absences
- anomalies
- dependencies
- system outputs
- environmental cues
- state changes
- relationships
- weak traces
- reconstructed traces

Signals are not interpretations.

Signals are not decisions.

Signals are not authority.

---

# Signal Ecology

Signal ecology is the environment in which signals emerge, persist, amplify, attenuate, normalize, disappear, reconstruct, or become novel.

Signal ecology is dynamic.

Signals may influence the conditions in which other signals appear.

Signals may become easier or harder to localize over time.

Signal ecology is not a decision system.

Signal ecology is not governance.

---

# Observatory

The Observatory is a signal-localization and inspectability-preservation instrument operating within signal ecology.

The Observatory preserves:

- signal localization
- inspectability
- localization capacity
- distinction visibility
- formation visibility

The Observatory does not:

- interpret
- decide
- authorize
- govern
- execute
- optimize
- prescribe
- certify causality

---

# Formation Visibility

Formation visibility is the observer-visible condition where signals, relations, absences, or patterns become inspectable as a formation.

Formation visibility is not meaning.

Formation visibility is not authority.

Formation visibility is not decision support.

Formation visibility is bounded inspectability.

---

# External Interpretation

Interpretation occurs outside the Observatory.

External actors may interpret observable formations based on context, values, objectives, authority, domain expertise, or institutional responsibility.

The Observatory does not perform interpretation.

The Observatory does not own interpretation.

---

# Decision, Action, and Consequence

Decision, action, and consequence remain outside the Observatory.

The Observatory stops before decision.

The Observatory stops before action.

The Observatory stops before consequence.

The Observatory may preserve visibility into formations.

It does not convert visibility into authority.

---

# Signal States

Signals may appear in multiple states:

- visible signal
- weak signal
- distributed signal
- normalized signal
- lost signal
- reconstructed signal
- novel signal

Signal states are observer-visible descriptors.

They are not truth claims.

They are not causality claims.

They are not authority claims.

---
