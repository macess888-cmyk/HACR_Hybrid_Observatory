# HACR Hybrid Observatory
# Runtime Drift and Continuation

Continuation Persistence / Drift Surfaces / Delayed Survivability

---

## Purpose

This document identifies conditions where operational continuation may survive, drift, redistribute, or remain effect-capable despite visible interruption, refusal, invalidity, or apparent stabilization.

Runtime interruption does not guarantee continuation collapse.

---

## Core Principle

Operational continuation may survive beyond visible interruption.

Formal invalidity is not guaranteed operational non-survivability.

---

## Runtime Drift Pattern

Continuation may drift through:

- stale state
- replay inheritance
- delayed synchronization
- retries/fallback
- downstream persistence
- distributed topology
- operational memory
- institutional habit
- manual reconstruction
- hidden carriers
- residue survivability
- topology redistribution
- delayed execution surfaces

These may preserve continuation capability after local interruption.

---

## Drift != Legitimacy

Continuation persistence does not imply:

- admissibility
- legitimacy
- correctness
- governance validity
- operational closure

Runtime survivability may persist independently of formal status.

---

## Delayed Survivability

A consequence may appear interrupted while continuation remains:

- delayed
- queued
- replayable
- recoverable
- redistributable
- manually reconstructable
- operationally remembered
- downstream reachable

Visible pause is not guaranteed collapse.

---

## Operational Momentum

Continuation may preserve momentum through:

- retries
- synchronization lag
- workflow familiarity
- cached assumptions
- historical routing
- delayed processing
- distributed persistence
- institutional continuity pressure

Operational momentum is not admissibility.

---

## Drift Surfaces

Potential runtime drift surfaces include:

- asynchronous infrastructure
- queues
- caches
- retries
- replicas
- offline systems
- downstream consumers
- support workflows
- institutional memory
- operator continuation
- replay systems
- stale escalation paths

---

## Fragmented Drift

Fragmented continuation remains continuation.

Continuation may survive partially through:

- fragmented workflows
- distributed memory
- stale replicas
- replay fragments
- partial downstream persistence
- human reconstruction
- residue inheritance

Aggregate collapse requires aggregate interruption.

---

## HOLD Conditions

Route to HOLD if:

- continuation drift visibility is incomplete
- replay inheritance remains unresolved
- downstream persistence is unclear
- synchronization timing is unknown
- stale state remains reachable
- topology redistribution is unresolved
- operational momentum may survive interruption

Unknown drift surfaces remain operationally equivalent to survivable continuation.

---

## Diagnostic Boundary

The observatory may expose runtime drift conditions.

It may not determine:

- legal validity
- governance legitimacy
- semantic truth
- institutional correctness
- operational admissibility

---

## Final Constraint

Operational continuation may survive beyond visible interruption.

Local interruption is not guaranteed aggregate collapse.

Stale continuation remains continuation until proven inert.
