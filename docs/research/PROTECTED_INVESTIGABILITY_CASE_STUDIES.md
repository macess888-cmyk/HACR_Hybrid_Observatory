# Case Study 01 — Galileo

Question:

Can heliocentrism be examined as a valid description of reality?

Background:

The dispute surrounding Galileo is often described as a conflict between science and religion. While that framing captures part of the historical tension, it may not fully explain what occurred.

For purposes of this research, the question is narrower:

Were the conditions required for continued examination preserved?

Investigability Status:

Contested

Observed Conditions:

Present:

* Observations existed.
* Competing explanations existed.
* Evidence gathering continued.
* Alternative models could be formulated.

Restricted:

* Certain conclusions encountered institutional resistance.
* Examination and consequence became increasingly coupled.
* Inquiry and authority became difficult to separate.

Potential Failure Modes:

Closure

Question
↓
Restricted Examination

Capture

Question
↓
Institutional Consequence

Consequence Coupling

Question
↓
Investigation
↓
Immediate Personal or Institutional Consequence

Effect:

Investigability may contract even when observation remains available.

Observations:

The central issue may not have been whether observations existed.

The central issue may have been whether examination could continue independently of authority structures.

This distinction is important because:

Observation
≠
Protected Investigability

A signal may be visible while examination of that signal becomes constrained.

Candidate Relevance:

Protected Investigability may explain part of the conflict if:

* observations remained possible,
* but continued examination became increasingly constrained.

# Case Study 02 — Darwin

Question:

Can biological diversity be explained through natural selection?

Background:

Darwin presents a different pressure surface from Galileo.

The primary question is not whether investigation was permitted.

The primary question is whether investigation was allowed to continue long enough before evaluation and publication occurred.

Darwin spent decades gathering observations, comparing evidence, refining explanations, and delaying publication.

For purposes of this research, the question becomes:

What conditions allowed extended investigation without premature evaluation?

Investigability Status:

Appears Strong

Observed Conditions:

Present:

* Long-duration evidence gathering.
* Multiple sources of observation.
* Continuous refinement of explanatory models.
* Delayed commitment to publication.
* Ongoing comparison of competing explanations.

Protected Conditions:

* Time for evidence accumulation.
* Ability to revise conclusions.
* Ability to delay evaluation.
* Continued reality contact through observation.

Potential Failure Modes:

Premature Evaluation

Question
↓
Insufficient Investigation
↓
Commitment

Effect:

Weak conclusions may become fixed before evidence matures.

Closure

Question
↓
Conclusion Fixed Too Early

Effect:

Alternative explanations receive insufficient examination.

Consequence Coupling

Question
↓
Investigation
↓
Immediate Social Consequence

Effect:

Investigability contracts before evidence accumulation is complete.

Observations:

Darwin appears to illustrate a different failure surface than Galileo.

Galileo pressures:

Investigability
vs
Authority

Darwin pressures:

Investigability
vs
Time

The extended duration of investigation may have contributed significantly to the strength of later evaluation.

This raises an important question:

Did delayed evaluation improve reliability?

Or did reliable evaluation simply require longer investigability?

Candidate Relevance:

Protected Investigability may explain part of the case if:

* investigation remained active,
* commitment remained deferrable,
* evidence continued accumulating,
* evaluation occurred after substantial constraint emerged.

OPEN

Further historical review required.
