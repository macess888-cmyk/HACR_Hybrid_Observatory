# Candidate A

Protected Investigability

Core Question:

What conditions allow meaningful investigation to continue?

Strengths:

* Directly addresses preservation of inquiry.
* Explains Galileo pressure surfaces.
* Explains investigation restrictions.
* Explains examination protection mechanisms.

Weaknesses:

* Current cases often contain successful investigation.
* May explain fewer downstream effects.
* May not explain execution failures.

Open Questions:

* Is investigability the primary object?
* Does protection remain central across all cases?

Status:

OPEN

---

# Candidate B

Examination Compression

Core Question:

What reduces, redirects, constrains, or disconnects examination?

Strengths:

* Appears across all reviewed cases.
* Explains authority pressure.
* Explains premature evaluation.
* Explains institutional compression.
* Explains execution decoupling.

Weaknesses:

* Compression may be descriptive rather than causal.
* Compression may merely describe outcomes.
* Compression may not identify the underlying mechanism.

Open Questions:

* Is compression a real object?
* Is compression an effect rather than a cause?

Status:

OPEN

---

# Candidate C

Pressure Atlas

Core Question:

What pressure surfaces repeatedly act upon investigation?

Strengths:

* Organizes cross-case observations.
* Preserves multiple pressure classes.
* Supports comparative analysis.
* Explains diversity of observed constraints.

Weaknesses:

* Pressure categories may be arbitrary.
* Atlas may be classification rather than explanation.
* Pressure may only indicate where effects become visible.

Open Questions:

* Does pressure have explanatory value?
* Can pressure surfaces be reduced further?

Status:

OPEN
