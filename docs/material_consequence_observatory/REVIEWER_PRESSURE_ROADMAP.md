## Roadmap Step 1: Clarify Geometry Status

Current issue:

The term "Invariant Registry" may imply stronger claims than the current method supports.

Possible classification:

```text
Observed Survivor
↓
Candidate Geometry
↓
Falsifiable Structural Claim
↓
Stabilized Invariant
```

No geometry should be treated as fully stabilized before it survives counterexample testing.

---

## Roadmap Step 2: Add Interaction-Stress-Divergence Coupling

Current geometry:

```text
Condition
↓
Interaction
↓
Consequence
↓
Persistence
↓
Recoverability
↓
Reference Integrity
```

Missing layer:

```text
Interaction
↓
Stress Pattern
↓
Divergence Geometry
```

Core question:

Which interaction types tend to produce which divergence geometries?

---

## Roadmap Step 3: Define Transition Logic

Current gap:

The framework identifies divergence but does not yet define transition criteria.

Questions:

* When does presence become integrity loss?
* When does symptom removal fail to restore condition?
* When does continuity stop indicating health?
* When does persistence outlive function?
* When does representation stop corresponding to reality condition?

This does not require authority.

It requires inspection criteria.

---

## Roadmap Step 4: Add Counterexample Testing

The next phase should deliberately search for:

* cases where geometries fail
* cases where geometries collapse into each other
* cases where one geometry masks another
* cases where the framework over-applies
* domains where the framework becomes weak or meaningless

Failure cases are useful results.

---

## Roadmap Step 5: Inspect Earlier Divergence

Marianna's pressure suggests a deeper layer:

```text
Condition
≠
Formation Preconditions
```

A condition may remain observable while the prerequisites that allow it to continue emerging have already degraded.

Candidate inspection path:

```text
Formation Preconditions
↓
Precondition Drift
↓
Condition Persists
↓
Delayed Divergence
↓
Visible Consequence
```

This may become a new geometry class if it survives pressure.

---

* transition logic
* counterexample testing
