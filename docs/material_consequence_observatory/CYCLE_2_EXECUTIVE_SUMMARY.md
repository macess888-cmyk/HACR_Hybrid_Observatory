# Executive Summary

Cycle 2 began with a simple question:

```text
Which survivor best explains continuation?
```

The repository investigated whether continuation is primarily explained by:

```text
Localization

Review

Adaptation

Preservation

Coupling
```

using historical pressure cases.

The objective was to determine whether one survivor consistently explains why some entities are considered continuations of earlier entities while others are not.

---

# Historical Audits

Cycle 2 evaluated:

```text
Byzantine Continuation

Roman Catholic Church Continuation

Jewish Diaspora Continuation

Chinese Civilization Continuation

Japan Post-WWII Continuation

Soviet Union Dissolution

Latin Language Dissolution

Ship of Theseus
```

These cases were selected because they exhibit different combinations of:

```text
Transformation

Replacement

Adaptation

Preservation

Continuity

Discontinuity
```

---

# Primary Observation

No survivor consistently explained continuation.

Examples included:

```text
Preservation
without clear continuation.
```

```text
Adaptation
without continuation.
```

```text
Replacement
with continuation.
```

```text
Transformation
with continuation.
```

As pressure accumulated, survivor-based explanations became increasingly incomplete.

---

# Research Transition

The repository gradually shifted from:

```text
What survives?
```

to:

```text
What qualifies as continuation?
```

This produced a new research object:

```text
Continuation Admissibility
```

The focus moved away from identifying a winning survivor and toward understanding why continuation judgments are admitted at all.

---

# Emergent Findings

Historical pressure repeatedly produced:

```text
Continuation
↓
Admissibility
↓
Determination
↓
Standing
```

Observation:

Continuation increasingly appeared to behave as a determination problem rather than a survivor problem.

Boundary cases such as:

```text
Ship of Theseus

Soviet Union

Latin
```

were particularly important because they exposed disagreements about continuation despite visible continuity surfaces.

---

# Emergent Primitive

The concept that repeatedly reappeared throughout the cycle was:

```text
Determination
```

The repository increasingly encountered questions such as:

```text
What makes a continuation determination admissible?

What grants a determination standing?

Can determination be separated from authority?
```

These questions were not part of the original Cycle 2 objective.

# Final Assessment

Cycle 2 began as:

```text
Continuation Research
```

and ended as:

```text
Continuation Admissibility Research.
```

The cycle did not answer:

```text

```

```text
What makes a continuation determination admissible without granting authority?
```

This transition represents the primary outcome of Cycle 2.
