## Overview

Cycle 2 began as an investigation into continuation.

The original objective was to determine whether a single survivor consistently explains why some entities are treated as continuations of earlier entities while others are not.

Historical pressure was applied through a series of continuation and dissolution audits, including:

* Byzantine Continuation
* Roman Catholic Church Continuation
* Jewish Diaspora Continuation
* Chinese Civilization Continuation
* Japan Post-WWII Continuation
* Soviet Union Dissolution
* Latin Language Dissolution
* Ship of Theseus

---

## Initial Research Question

Which survivor best explains continuation?

Candidate survivors included:

* Localization
* Review
* Adaptation
* Preservation
* Coupling

---

## Primary Finding

No tested survivor consistently explained continuation across all audited cases.

Observed patterns included:

* Preservation without clear continuation
* Adaptation without continuation
* Replacement with continuation
* Transformation with continuation

Result:

No survivor emerged as a sufficient standalone explanation.

---

## Research Transition

As pressure accumulated, the repository shifted from:

"What survives?"

to:

"What qualifies as continuation?"

and ultimately toward:

"What makes a continuation determination admissible?"

This transition produced a new research object:

Continuation Admissibility.

---

## What Cycle 2 Established

* No tested survivor appears sufficient to explain continuation on its own.
* Continuation Admissibility emerged as a meaningful research object.
* Determination repeatedly emerged across independent investigations.
* Standing repeatedly emerged as a boundary concept.
* Observation–Authority separation appeared repeatedly across repository branches.

---

## What Cycle 2 Did Not Establish

* A definitive continuation survivor.
* Formal continuation criteria.
* Formal determination criteria.
* Formal standing criteria.
* Observation ≠ Authority as a proven invariant.
* Repository convergence as an established fact.
