# Executive Summary

The Signal Surfacer branch has completed its first exploratory cycle.

The branch began as an investigation into hidden, fragmented, concealed, distorted, and inherited signals.

The branch did not terminate at signal analysis.

Current assessment:

The branch is no longer centered on signal localization.

The branch is increasingly centered on relationship preservation, identity persistence, constitutive coupling, and dependency structures.

# Major Reductions Produced

## Reduction 1

Signal Surface
!=
Signal Origin

The branch repeatedly suggested that signal visibility and signal origin may occur at different locations.

Signal Surface may localize where degradation becomes visible rather than where degradation begins.

---

## Reduction 2

Object Preservation
!=
Relationship Preservation

Objects may remain visible.

Relationships may weaken.

Meaning appears to change within relationships before objects visibly degrade.

---

## Reduction 3

Descriptive Relationships
!=
Constitutive Relationships

Not all relationships appear equally important.

Some relationships describe an object.

Others appear to participate in defining the object.

---

## Reduction 4

Identity Persistence

An object may survive loss of descriptive relationships.

An object may not survive loss of constitutive relationships.

Open question:

What must survive for an object to remain itself?

---

## Reduction 5

Evidence Locality

Evidence may continue accumulating while constitutive relationships weaken.

Confidence may increase during degradation.

Potential distinction:

Evidence Of Object Persistence

vs

Evidence Of Identity Persistence

---

## Reduction 6

Dependency Structures

Possible deeper primitive emerged.

Potential chain:

Dependency
→ Relationship
→ Identity
→ Object

This remains unresolved.
