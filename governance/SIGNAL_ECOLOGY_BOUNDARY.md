# Signal Ecology Boundary

## Purpose

This document defines the boundary around signal ecology within the HACR Hybrid Observatory.

Signal ecology describes the environment in which observable traces emerge, persist, weaken, normalize, disappear, reconstruct, or become novel.

The Observatory may preserve visibility into signal ecology.

The Observatory does not interpret signal ecology.

UNKNOWN → HOLD.

---

# Boundary Statement

Signal ecology is observable.

Signal ecology is not authority.

Signal ecology is not governance.

Signal ecology is not prediction.

Signal ecology is not decision support.

Signal ecology is not execution.

---

# Signal Boundary

Signals are observable traces.

Signals are not meanings.

Signals are not conclusions.

Signals are not decisions.

Signals are not instructions.

Signals are not proof of causality.

---

# Localization Boundary

Localization identifies where a signal, trace, relation, absence, or formation can be inspected.

Localization does not establish meaning.

Localization does not establish priority.

Localization does not establish authority.

Localization does not establish required action.

---

# Inspectability Boundary

Inspectability preserves the ability to examine, verify, correlate, contextualize, and reconstruct signals over time.

Inspectability is not control.

Inspectability is not intervention.

Inspectability is not enforcement.

Inspectability is not certification.

---

# Formation Visibility Boundary

Formation visibility preserves the visibility of emerging or persistent formations.

Formation visibility does not determine what those formations mean.

Formation visibility does not decide whether they matter.

Formation visibility does not prescribe response.

---

# External Interpretation Boundary

Interpretation occurs outside the Observatory.

External actors may interpret formations.

External actors may make decisions.

External actors may take action.

The Observatory does not cross that boundary.

---

# Prohibited Conflations

Do not conflate:

- signal with meaning
- localization with interpretation
- visibility with authority
- inspectability with control
- formation visibility with decision support
- observation with execution
- signal ecology with governance

---

# Research Boundary

The current research question is:

Does preserving inspectability preserve future localization capacity, or can observability structures themselves gradually shape which future distinctions remain localizable?

This question remains observational.

It does not authorize intervention.
