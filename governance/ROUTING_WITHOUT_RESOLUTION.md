# ROUTING WITHOUT RESOLUTION

## PURPOSE

This note clarifies a strict observatory boundary:

The observatory may route, expose, preserve, localize, or inspect signals without resolving, validating, legitimizing, optimizing, or operationalizing them.

Routing preserves visibility.

Routing does not preserve correctness.

Routing does not inherit authority.

Routing does not imply admissibility.

---

# CORE REDUCTION

Signal transport != signal legitimacy.

Docking != governance.

Visibility != operational authorization.

Propagation != admissibility.

Observation != execution.

---

# OBSERVATORY POSITION

The observatory:

- may expose continuity structures,
- may reveal directional survivability,
- may localize propagation pathways,
- may preserve bounded replay visibility,
- may inspect topology-local stabilization behavior,

while remaining:

- observer-only,
- non-authoritative,
- non-operational,
- non-executing,
- fail-closed.

---

# ROUTING BOUNDARY

The observatory may:

- hold signals,
- preserve inspectability,
- classify continuity states,
- expose topology relationships,
- maintain bounded replay visibility,
- preserve directional observability.

The observatory may not:

- resolve disputes,
- validate correctness,
- assign legitimacy,
- optimize operational paths,
- arbitrate execution,
- escalate authority,
- convert routing into governance.

---

# PROPAGATION GATING

Entry may be observable without unrestricted propagation being permitted.

Propagation remains:

- bounded,
- localized,
- inspectable,
- interruption-compatible,
- topology-constrained.

The observatory does not assume:

- global interoperability,
- unrestricted continuity,
- inevitability,
- automatic inheritance,
- operational convergence.

---

# FAILURE LOCALIZATION

Local routing failure does not imply systemic collapse.

Continuity interruption may remain:

- local,
- bounded,
- compartmentalized,
- directionally constrained.

The observatory therefore avoids:

- totalizing system claims,
- inevitability framing,
- universal propagation assumptions,
- irreversible continuity assertions.
