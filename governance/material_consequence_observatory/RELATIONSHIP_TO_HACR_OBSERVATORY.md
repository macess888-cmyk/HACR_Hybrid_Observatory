# Relationship to HACR Observatory

## Status

Material Consequence Observatory

Relationship Architecture Definition

## Purpose

This document defines the relationship between the Material Consequence Observatory and the broader HACR Hybrid Observatory.

The Material Consequence Observatory is not a separate governance system.

It is not a certification system.

It is not a material discovery system.

It is not a capability-generation system.

It is an observer-only extension of the broader observability posture.

UNKNOWN → HOLD.

---

## HACR Observatory Core Question

The HACR Hybrid Observatory asks:

> Can important operational properties diverge before visible consequence emerges?

The Observatory improves visibility into:

* continuity
* interruption
* recoverability
* signal ecology
* formation visibility
* reference preservation

without crossing into authority, decision, execution, or governance.

---

## Material Consequence Observatory Core Question

The Material Consequence Observatory asks:

> Can important material, ecological, human, or infrastructural consequences diverge before visible consequence emerges?

It improves visibility into:

* degradation
* exposure
* persistence
* dependency
* recoverability
* consequence accumulation

without crossing into synthesis, optimization, deployment, or capability generation.

Both remain observer-only.

## Relationship to Formation Visibility

Formation Visibility asks:

> What formations become observable?

Material Consequence Visibility asks:

> What consequence formations become observable?

Examples:

* corrosion
* waste accumulation
* persistence
* infrastructure degradation
* exposure chains
* dependency chains

Material consequence inspection extends formation visibility into physical systems.

## Relationship to Continuity

The HACR Observatory established:

```text
Continuity
≠
Recoverability
```

The Material Consequence Observatory extends this observation.

A material may:

* remain present
* remain recognizable
* remain documented

while:

* reliability degrades
* exposure accumulates
* recoverability decreases

Material continuity does not guarantee preserved condition.

---

## Relationship to Recoverability

Recoverability asks:

> Can the condition be restored?

Material consequence inspection asks:

> What is making restoration harder?

Examples:

* corrosion
* contamination
* persistence
* hidden degradation
* accumulated dependency

Recoverability remains a separate inspection surface.

---

## Relationship to Reference Preservation

Reference Preservation asks:

> Is the determination still about the same thing?

Material consequence inspection introduces a physical analogue.

Examples:

* Bridge remains visible.
* Bridge remains documented.
* Bridge remains classified as operational.

Yet:

* Structural condition may have changed.
* Material integrity may have degraded.
* Represented condition and actual condition may diverge.

This creates a material reference-preservation problem.
