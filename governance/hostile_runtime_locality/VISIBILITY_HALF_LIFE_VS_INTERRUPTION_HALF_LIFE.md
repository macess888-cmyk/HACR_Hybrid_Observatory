# VISIBILITY HALF-LIFE VS INTERRUPTION HALF-LIFE

## Core Reduction

visibility half-life
>
interruption half-life

Modern systems may preserve visible coherence longer than they preserve executable interruption realism.

This means a runtime may remain:
- observable
- explainable
- reportable
- replayable
- escalatable-looking
- procedurally coherent
- and operationally smooth

after executable interruption can no longer materially affect consequence locally.

---

## Runtime Concern

Visibility may decay slower than interruption viability.

A system can continue showing:
- dashboards
- telemetry
- alerts
- review queues
- escalation paths
- logs
- replay traces
- governance artifacts

while the practical ability to interrupt consequence has already degraded.

This creates a dangerous representational asymmetry:

the system still appears governable
because representation survives,
not because executable consequence locality remains reachable.

---

## Hostile-Runtime Interpretation

Under hostile runtime conditions, continuation may harden faster than interruption can arrive.

This may occur through:
- synchronization completion
- dependency accumulation
- workflow normalization
- reconstruction pathway stabilization
- escalation delay
- locality fragmentation
- operational familiarity
- telemetry preservation
- and representational smoothing.

The result is a widening gap between:

what remains visible

and

what remains executable as interruption.

## Observatory Focus

The observatory does not determine whether a system is:
- safe
- legitimate
- controllable
- governable
- resilient
- or operationally acceptable.

The observatory only inspects whether visibility has outlived executable interruption realism under bounded runtime conditions.
