# INVARIANT_CORRIDOR_CONTRACTION_NOTE

## Purpose

This note defines bounded corridor contraction as an observer-safe continuity regulation primitive.

The objective is not containment escalation.

The objective is preservation of invariant coherence under measurable stress while maintaining:

- recoverability
- bounded admissible freedom
- smooth re-expansion
- non-punitive contraction
- observability without authority substitution

---

# Core Reduction

A system may contract admissible operational range under stress without collapsing into irreversible containment.

The corridor may narrow.

The invariant must remain recoverable unless the invariant itself is structurally violated.

---

# Corridor Definition

Define a bounded admissible corridor:

C(t) = { x | L(t) ≤ x ≤ U(t) }

where:

- L(t) = lower admissible bound
- U(t) = upper admissible bound

The corridor width is:

Δ(t) = U(t) - L(t)

---

# Contraction Principle

Under measurable degradation:

- coherence loss
- resource strain
- instability accumulation
- adversarial disturbance
- synchronization failure

the corridor contracts proportionally:

Δ(t + Δt) < Δ(t)

without requiring immediate termination.

---

# Non-Collapse Requirement

Corridor contraction must remain bounded:

Δ(t) ≥ Δ_min

A corridor that collapses to zero under ordinary perturbation is operationally brittle.

Non-collapse preserves:

- reversibility
- recovery viability
- graceful degradation
- bounded continuation observability

---

# Recovery Principle

If coherence returns and invariant conditions stabilize:

Δ(t + Δt) → Δ_max

Recovery must remain:

- smooth
- measurable
- non-oscillatory
- non-authoritarian

The objective is not forced normalization.

The objective is recoverable operational continuity.

---

# Pause As Governance Primitive

A system incapable of pausing before propagation cannot claim alignment under pressure.

Pause is not failure.

Pause is admissible stabilization.

The following are valid observatory outcomes:

- HOLD
- HALT
- DELAY
- RANGE CONTRACTION
- RECOVERY PENDING

without requiring escalation into terminal failure semantics.

---

# Bounds Versus Cages

Bounds preserve freedom when they are:

- explicit
- minimal
- revisable
- recoverable
- measurable

Bounds become cages when they are:

- opaque
- irreversible
- authority-absolute
- non-recoverable
- non-negotiable

---

# Co-Alignment Across Scale

Co-alignment across scale occurs when independently bounded systems can interact under shared invariants without requiring:

- centralized domination
- enforced synchronization
- irreversible containment
- hierarchy-dependent admissibility

The objective is not uniformity.

The objective is coherent interoperability under shared constraints.
