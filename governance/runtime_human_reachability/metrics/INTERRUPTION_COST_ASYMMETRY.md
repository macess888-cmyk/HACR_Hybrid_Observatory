# Interruption Cost Asymmetry

Interruption Cost Asymmetry inspects whether the operational burden required to materially interrupt consequence exceeds practical interruption reachability.

The observatory does not inspect:

- politics
- morality
- institutional blame
- legitimacy
- social theory

The object is strictly runtime-local interruption burden.

Primary question:

Can humans still materially interrupt consequence before interruption burden exceeds practical recovery capability?

Measured pressure surfaces may include:

- coordination burden
- interruption timing burden
- interruption reconstruction cost
- synchronization overhead
- operational recovery effort
- interruption sustainment cost

If interruption burden cannot be materially measured:

UNKNOWN -> HOLD

If continuation persistence remains operationally cheap while interruption becomes practically unreachable:

FAIL

If interruption remains materially reachable and sustainable:

PASS

Core locks:

- Asymmetry does not imply destiny.
- Interruption burden is not legitimacy.
- Operational persistence is not authority.
- Measurement is not admissibility.
- UNKNOWN -> HOLD.
