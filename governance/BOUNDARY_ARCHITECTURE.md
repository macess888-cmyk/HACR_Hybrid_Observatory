# Boundary Architecture

## Purpose

This document describes the high-level relationship between major repository boundaries.

The purpose of the boundary architecture is not to govern execution, authorize outcomes, certify correctness, or establish authority.

The purpose is to provide observer-visible structure for understanding how repository boundaries interact.

UNKNOWN → HOLD.

---

# Architectural Principle

Boundaries exist to preserve distinction.

When distinctions collapse, interpretation drift increases.

The boundary architecture exists to maintain separation between concepts that may appear related but serve different purposes.

Examples:

* visibility is not enforcement
* observability is not authority
* continuity is not legitimacy
* evidence is not consequence
* representation is not reality

# Reality Boundary

Question:

What exists independent of representation?

Purpose:

Preserve distinction between reality and description.

Failure Surface:

Mistaking representations for reality.

---

# Evidence Boundary

Question:

What can be observed?

Purpose:

Preserve distinction between observation and assumption.

Failure Surface:

Treating assumptions as evidence.

---

# Interpretation Boundary

Question:

How is evidence understood?

Purpose:

Preserve distinction between evidence and meaning.

Failure Surface:

Confusing interpretation with observation.

---

# Trust Boundary

Question:

What is relied upon?

Purpose:

Preserve distinction between observation and reliance.

Failure Surface:

Treating trust as proof.

---

# Consequence Boundary

Question:

What produces effect?

Purpose:

Preserve distinction between observation and outcome.

Failure Surface:

Confusing visibility with consequence formation.

---

# Dependency Boundary

Question:

What must remain available?

Purpose:

Preserve distinction between consequence and support structure.

Failure Surface:

Hidden dependency accumulation.

---

# Continuity Boundary

Question:

What survives interruption?

Purpose:

Preserve distinction between existence and persistence.

Failure Surface:

Assuming continuation without inspection.

---

# Failure Boundary

Question:

Where does viability degrade?

Purpose:

Preserve distinction between operation and survivability.

Failure Surface:

Visible operation masking degradation.

---

# Unknown Boundary

Question:

What remains outside observation?

Purpose:

Preserve distinction between visibility and certainty.

Failure Surface:

Treating absence of evidence as evidence of absence.

---

# Architectural Relationships

Reality supports observation.

Observation supports evidence.

Evidence supports interpretation.

Interpretation influences trust.

Trust influences consequence.

Consequence depends on dependencies.

Dependencies influence continuity.

Continuity influences survivability.

Survivability influences failure visibility.

Unknown remains beyond current visibility.

These relationships are observational.

They are not causal claims.

They are not governance claims.

They are not authority claims.

---

# Boundary Invariant

No single boundary explains the entire system.

Each boundary answers a different question.

Collapsing boundaries increases interpretation risk.

Preserving distinctions increases observer clarity.

---

This architecture is descriptive.

It is not prescriptive.
